Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about EER and BASIX reports

What is an EER report and do I need one in Canberra?
An EER (Energy Efficiency Rating) report measures a home's thermal performance on a scale of 0 to 10 stars. In the ACT, an EER report is required when selling a residential property, and a minimum 6-star rating is needed for new builds and major renovations. Blast Energy provides NatHERS accredited EER assessments for Canberra and surrounding areas.

What is a BASIX report and where is it required?
A BASIX (Building Sustainability Index) report is a NSW-specific requirement that ensures new residential developments meet sustainability targets for water, energy and thermal comfort. It is mandatory before a Construction Certificate or Complying Development Certificate is issued in New South Wales. Blast Energy prepares BASIX reports exclusively for NSW projects.

How long does it take to get an EER report?
Turnaround depends on property complexity, but most EER reports are completed within 3 to 5 business days after the site assessment. Blast Energy also offers expedited services for urgent property sales or development approvals.

What is NatHERS accreditation?
NatHERS (Nationwide House Energy Rating Scheme) is the Australian government framework that sets the standard for rating residential energy efficiency. A NatHERS accredited assessor uses approved software to model a home's heating and cooling energy use. Blast Energy assessors hold current NatHERS accreditation.

How much does an EER or BASIX report cost?
Costs vary based on property size, type and complexity. Contact Blast Energy for a personalised quote — we offer competitive pricing with no hidden fees and can usually provide a quote within 24 hours.

What is the minimum star rating required for a new home in the ACT?
New residential buildings in the ACT must achieve a minimum 6-star energy efficiency rating under NatHERS. This applies to both new constructions and major renovations. Blast Energy can assess your plans early in the design stage to ensure compliance and avoid costly redesigns.

Do I need an EER report to sell my house in Canberra?
Yes. Under ACT legislation, sellers of residential properties are required to disclose the property's Energy Efficiency Rating as part of the sale process. The EER must be included in the contract documentation. Blast Energy provides fast-turnaround EER reports to help you meet settlement deadlines.

What is the difference between an EER report and a BASIX report?
An EER report rates a home's thermal (heating and cooling) performance on a 0–10 star scale and is primarily used in the ACT. A BASIX report is a NSW-specific compliance tool that covers three areas — water, energy and thermal comfort — and is required before construction approval in NSW. Blast Energy offers both services.

Can I improve my home's EER star rating?
Yes. Common upgrades include adding or upgrading insulation (ceiling, wall and underfloor), installing double-glazed windows, sealing air gaps and draughts, and improving shading with eaves or external blinds. Blast Energy provides actionable recommendations with every EER report to help you boost your rating cost-effectively.

What documents do I need to provide for an EER or BASIX assessment?
For an EER report, we typically need architectural floor plans, a site plan showing orientation, and any specifications for insulation, glazing and construction materials. For a BASIX report, we also require details on proposed water fixtures, hot water systems and any renewable energy systems. Blast Energy will guide you through exactly what is needed.

Does Blast Energy service areas outside Canberra?
Yes. While we are based in Canberra, Blast Energy provides EER report services across Australia and BASIX reports for all NSW projects. Remote assessments can often be completed using architectural plans without requiring an on-site visit.

What happens if my property does not meet the minimum EER star rating?
If your property falls below the required rating, Blast Energy will provide a detailed list of recommended improvements — such as better insulation, upgraded glazing or improved sealing — along with the estimated star-rating gain for each change, so you can prioritise the most cost-effective upgrades.
